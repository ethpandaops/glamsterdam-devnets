Optimistic recovery delays during rollout and live operation

During the release rollout, Teku 26.10.0 on the Geth and Erigon pairings followed the beacon head but remained optimistic for several minutes and skipped validator attestations. The ELs continued importing canonical execution blocks while Engine interactions returned SYNCING. During later read-only monitoring, both Teku–Erigon and Lodestar–Erigon temporarily returned to optimism without a restart. This is worth a focused synchronization and recovery test with the consensus and execution-client teams; the current evidence does not identify a single faulty component.

Further investigation: recurring Erigon recovery delays, not an isolated rollout event

Issue type: synchronization / performance investigation. Owners: Erigon Engine/execution, with Teku and Lodestar interoperability input. Suggested priority: medium pending controlled isolation. Reproduction mode: live recurrence plus source/timing trace; no fresh controlled reorg replay.

The follow-up case-insensitive overnight query finds 22 distinct Teku–Erigon warnings activating optimistic sync, from 00:36:36 to 07:23:02 UTC. These are logged activations, not 22 measured outage durations or a validator-duty census. No matching Lodestar rows appear in that query; do not infer that both nodes shared every later episode. The 08:18 direct check shows both pairings recovered and their execution containers still had zero restarts.

Lodestar–Erigon independently logs the same unwind and replacement hash. Its replacement newPayload starts at 22:58:37.120 and returns quick SYNCING at 22:58:37.628, approximately 508 ms later.

In release commit 8c1e3893d77d230720d172f1492d95b5777a862c, the Engine handler checks quick status before full newPayload handling. Its readiness budget is 500 ms. It can return SYNCING when a parent is missing during download or when execution is not ready. The observed timings are consistent with the readiness budget, but neither branch identity nor the reason execution remains busy has been directly instrumented. Source presence and timing alone do not establish a cache defect.

The canonical chain context has execution 24455424 at slot 2975, 24455425 at 2976, no canonical beacon block at 2977, and replacement 24455426 at 2978. The intervening empty/late slot and unwind must be retained in a replay. Slot 2977 also had a late Grandine–Besu publication, documented separately; its absence from the canonical chain is not proof that its propagation alone caused Erigon's later delay.

Original occurrence and runtime evidence

Environment and impact

Both nodes have 1,000 validators and run the msf-2 Gloas and Amsterdam configuration, with 12-second slots and a 200-million-gas limit. The pairings are Teku 26.10.0 with Geth v1.17.8 and Teku 26.10.0 with Erigon v3.7.1. Images and validator ranges are recorded in the deployment pins and inventory.

The later Lodestar pairing reports Lodestar/v1.50.0/003bd66 and erigon/3.7.1/linux-amd64/go1.27.1. Its runtime command confirms discovery is disabled and three static shadowfork seed peers are configured. The direct samples recorded three EL peers on both Erigon pairings. Preserve this topology in a reproduction and compare it with broader peering when evaluating download delays.

The initial restart incidents below are historical rollout occurrences. The later recurrence was observed during read-only monitoring and was not induced by an operator intervention.

Observed sequence

All times are UTC on October 12, 2026. The rollout timeline preserves each held health check and the final recovery.

Pairing Observations

Unprompted recurrence during monitoring

At 22:58:37, both Teku–Erigon and Lodestar–Erigon started receiving SYNCING statuses. Erigon then requested backward downloads of recent parent/head blocks, completed small batches within a few seconds, and again needed additional recent blocks on later fork-choice calls. The bounded recurrence capture records the hashes, download completion, and subsequent VALID responses.

Teku skipped 31 attestations at slot 2982, logged at 22:59:24.124, as well as some aggregate, sync-contribution and payload-attestation duties during this interval. A 23:00 direct monitoring sample found Lodestar optimistic; the following minute's sample and independent follow-up checks found both pairings execution-valid at the same canonical root. Neither client was restarted or sent an operator fork-choice retry. This later episode recovered unaided and demonstrates that the liveness symptom is not confined to the rollout's restart sequence.

Lodestar's validator logs quantify proposer impact too: at 23:00:36, its slot-2988 produceBlockV4 request failed with HTTP 503, because the parent execution payload was not yet validated. The focused duty capture records the refusal and resumed payload attestations at slot 2989. The completed epoch-93 census independently identifies 2988 as a missing canonical slot, assigned to validator 28504 on this host.

The report does not infer a protocol violation merely from SYNCING while a parent is unavailable. The investigation should explain the repeated recent-parent downloads and lost duty time, including the order in which each CL supplies envelopes and retries engine calls.

The initial transition provides a more specific test condition: both ELs unwind from execution height 24455426 toward 24455424 while applying fork choice at height 24455425. A new payload for the replacement height-24455426 block arrives while that work is in progress and receives a quick SYNCING response. Independent canonical chain context identifies that replacement as slot 2978, hash 0xbd0610bd473b200aa2126362d3f5a310cde6aef72b7b5f3322c19d43fc518954, following the missing canonical slot 2977.

In Erigon v3.7.1's Engine server, a quick status can return before the supplied new payload reaches HandleNewPayload. The helper can report SYNCING for download/readiness conditions. This narrows the code and timing to instrument; the captured logs do not prove which helper branch fired, nor that returning SYNCING itself was wrong. A private test should overlap a fork-choice unwind with a replacement payload, then verify prompt import/retry and validator recovery once execution is ready.

What remains uncertain

Suggested priority: medium. Confidence: high in the skipped duties and prolonged optimistic state; medium in the recovery mechanism. The EL returning SYNCING for an unknown or in-flight head is not itself a protocol violation. The actionable question is whether the CL and EL make timely progress out of that state after a restart under this workload.

Several interventions occurred: authenticated fork-choice retries for already canonical execution heads, execution restarts, and—in the Erigon pairing—a checkpoint restore and additional beacon restart. A VALID response to an operator request does not prove the CL consumed that response. Recovery after these interventions cannot establish which one was necessary, or whether the node would eventually have recovered unaided.

Next client work

On a private restore, keep execution running and restart only the consensus client while the same workload and external builders continue. Trace each FCU and newPayload request, response, parent hash and retry. Check the CL's handling of pending optimistic envelopes and whether it retries or imports them when their execution parents become available. Repeat with both ELs, include the Lodestar–Erigon pairing, and compare a quiescent workload. Also run the live workload without a restart to cover the later recurrence. Use resumed validator duties and fully validated canonical heads as the success condition, rather than beacon head advancement alone.

The saved Erigon database failure has its own report; it must not be treated as proof of the cause of this broader liveness symptom. The post-rollout watcher leaves the live benchmark unchanged and does not perform the proposed restart experiments.
