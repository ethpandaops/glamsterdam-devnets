EL-01 · Heap exhaustion and slow payload requests cost proposals

Three Besu 26.9.0 processes exhausted their Java heap and restarted. Slot 2321 was missed during Teku–Besu payload timeouts; 2343 was a valid but late Lighthouse–Besu block on an old parent, then orphaned.

Besu has a 16 GiB maximum heap, G1 GC and ExitOnOutOfMemoryError on hosts with approximately 64 GB RAM. No container memory cap or matching kernel OOM kill was found. Five sampled JVMs use --cache-last-blocks=2048. Disk is 63% used, with 641–645 GB free. The OOM cause is confirmed; which objects retain the heap is not yet established.

Interpretation and limits

  • These are production observations and read-only reproduction checks. No isolated reproduction or source-level allocator diagnosis has been claimed.
  • Returned INVALID_PENALIZED(EXECUTION_INTERRUPTED) transaction-selection results and nonce-too-low RPC errors are not evidence of a consensus-invalid block. No persistent split was found in the current common-height check.
  • Dora’s execution timing display includes Nimbus EL responses while syncing. Those response times must not be treated as successful execution benchmarks. Timeouts also censor latency samples.
  • Large account-access payloads and historical eth_getLogs traffic were concurrent. Do not attribute every symptom to the workload without a controlled comparison.
  • Recent logs were inspected across all 41 hosts; detailed collection focused on affected nodes. Some high-volume Docker log samples were tail-bounded. This is not an exhaustive audit of every error since genesis.
  • All evidence times are UTC. Only selected public chain data, metrics and scrubbed log excerpts are embedded; raw Docker configuration and authentication material are excluded.
